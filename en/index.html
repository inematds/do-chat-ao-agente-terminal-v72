From chat to terminal: the agent working in a folder of your own

Open the terminal without fear, install the agent from the official source, set up the folder with rules, save every version with Git, keep your keys out of the text, and put your page online. Command by command, with what to check at each step.

The lessons

In the order you do them. Each lesson ends with practice and a short quiz; only move on to the next one when the "done when" is true.

  1. Lesson 1 · 15 minThe terminal starts by telling you where you areThe terminal is just another window into the same folders as File Explorer. You type a line, it sends one back. With six commands you know where you are, see what's there, create your projects folder, and move between folders without the mouse.
  2. Lesson 2 · 18 minInstall from the official source and sign in with your accountInstalling the program, signing in with your account, and opening it in the folder are three different steps, each with its own check. The command comes from the official page, the account is your subscription, and no password passes through the terminal.
  3. Lesson 3 · 16 minThe folder is the context: README, AGENTS.md and MarkdownWhen everything sits in a single folder, the AI reads material that has nothing to do with your request and you don't know where each sentence came from. One folder per subject, with one file for people and another for the agent, fixes this before the first request.
  4. Lesson 4 · 18 minFirst the agent reads, then it changes"Fix up the project" mixes diagnosis and change: if something goes wrong, you don't know which part it was. Separating reading from changing, giving the smallest permission that works and asking for a wrap-up that says what was checked turns the agent into someone you manage.
  5. Lesson 5 · 14 minSecrets don't go in the textA key pasted into a document, a screenshot, or a request becomes access for whoever reads it, and it stays valid after the conversation ends. Keeping the secret separate from everything else, before the first saved version, lets you share the folder without worry.
  6. Lesson 6 · 20 minGit: the safety net for undoing any changeIn a single task, the agent can change ten files at once. Without a history, you don't know what changed or how to go back. With Git, every good version is kept with a date and an explanation, and a change you didn't approve goes back with one command.
  7. Lesson 7 · 18 minAGENTS.md, Skills, and memory you can checkEvery new conversation with the agent starts from zero. Without an instructions file, you repeat the same warnings; with a vague instruction, nothing changes. A rule you can check, a routine saved in a Skill, and a short dated memory make the agent get better with each task.
  8. Lesson 8 · 20 minPage live and the same project on a VPSSaving, sending, and putting it live are three steps and three decisions. With an SSH key and gh, you send the project to GitHub, publish the page at an address that opens on your phone, and take the same folder to a VPS, where the agent works even when your computer is off.

Who it's for

  1. 1People who use ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini in the browser and have never opened a terminal.
  2. 2People who have already seen the program overview and want to really do step 1, with all the commands.
  3. 3People who will guide a team or a client and need to know how to check what the agent did.

What you'll be able to do

  1. 1Move around folders in cmd (and in the Mac or Linux Terminal) always knowing where you are.
  2. 2Install and sign in to Claude Code or Codex with your subscription, checking the method.
  3. 3Set up the agent's folder with README, AGENTS.md, .env kept out of Git, and a history you can undo.
  4. 4Publish the page on GitHub Pages and take the same project to a VPS.
